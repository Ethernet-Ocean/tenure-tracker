Question 1 · What did you learn about your professional performance from your student evaluation forms?

What I learned

Students told me that my preparation, individual support and career-focused teaching are working. DMA 15 and DMA 40 respondents were positive on all twelve items; all seven DMA 15 respondents strongly agreed that I was prepared, gave timely and helpful feedback, and cared about their success. In DMA 10, all seven respondents were positive on preparation, engagement, responding to questions, explaining grading and interest in their success.

Across all three courses, students valued my patience, hands-on technical help, in-class catch-up time, industry knowledge and internship opportunities. DMA 10 also showed me how to serve beginners even better: clearer, more visual demonstrations and more support to keep up with a course that intentionally moves at an industry pace across many tools.

Question 2 · What adjustments do you plan to make as a result of these evaluations?

What I have done and will do

Already done: I requested a better room for DMA 10 than K143, and when Canvas duplicated two weekly assignments I fixed them, excused the duplicates and told students right away.

What I found: a Canvas glitch had published many more duplicates in DMA 10 than students could see in the weekly modules: 120 published assignments for 16 weekly projects. I believe this was a significant contributing factor to the DMA 10 clarity results. DMA 15, which had no duplicates, rated clarity 7 of 7.

Two priorities for Spring 2027:

  1. Clearer instruction and course structure: a clean Canvas course with no published duplicate assignments, checked before each term and every week so students see only the 16 weekly projects (I already excuse any duplicate I find, so no student loses points); plus a posted agenda, written steps and a finished example beside each live demo, and positioning so every student can see and hear.
  2. Supporting beginners at an industry pace: the fast, multi-tool pace is intentional. It mirrors the real game-development pipeline, and industry professionals I have spoken with, including those offering internships, have confirmed it prepares students for the work. I will keep that pace and help beginners keep up: clear checkpoints, the matching video from my tutorial library in each week's module, and a short explanation of why each tool matters for their career.

I will measure progress with the Spring 2027 DMA 10 clarity and feedback items, a published-assignments count that matches the 16 weekly projects, and my next observation.

Supporting evidence

  • DMA 10 Canvas announcements “Week 7 Demonstrate – Canvas Update!” and “DMA 10 – Canvas Glitch Fixed” Canvas course record · in evaluation packet
