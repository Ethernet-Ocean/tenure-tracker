APPENDIX E-3 — RESPONSE TO STUDENT EVALUATIONS

The three questions below are the questions on the official Appendix E-3 form (Probationary Faculty Response to Student Evaluation Form, LBCCFA-LBCCD Master Agreement 2026–2029).

Q1 · What did you learn about your professional performance from your student evaluation forms? Q2 · What adjustments do you plan to make as a result of these evaluations? Q3 · What adjustments have you made as a result of previous evaluations?
About the data. Fall 2026 Individual Reports (created Nov 16, 2026): DMA 10 had 7 of 22 students respond (31.82%), DMA 15 7 of 25 (28%), and DMA 40 3 of 13 (23.08%). Responses are anonymous and the samples are small, so they describe only the students who responded. "Positive" means Strongly Agree or Agree. Every quotation below is copied exactly, including the student's spelling.

Q1 · WHAT DID I LEARN FROM MY STUDENT EVALUATIONS?

DMA 10 — INTRODUCTION TO GAME DESIGN (CRN 71864)

WHAT STUDENTS VALUED

  • Preparation, engagement and interest in student success: 7/7 positive (items 4, 8, 12). Responding to questions (item 10) and explaining the grading system (item 2) were also 7/7.
  • Patience: “He's very patient with us”
  • Going beyond: “Gives insightful advice on related subjects, explains why we learn certain things, goes above and beyond to help us out, and gives us lots of opportunities to succeed.”
  • Help, flexibility and catch-up time: “…He is flexible and provides many opportunities to his students to maintain good grades through devoting time in–class for catch up work, and provides technical assistance when needed, which many students often needed on a case–by–case basis.”
  • Industry knowledge and opportunities: “I say that he his knowledgeable video game design and the industry and even give us opportunity for people to internship in game industry.”
  • Explanations: “able to explain subjects in detail to make it more understandable” · “Commands the room extremely well. Breaks down confusing concepts. Accommodating in tricky situations.”

WHAT STUDENTS FOUND DIFFICULT

  • Clarity: item 6 (subject matter presented clearly) was 4/7 positive, with 3 Disagree. “make sure that people are able to be on the same page and able to see the instructions clearly”
  • Feedback timeliness: item 9 was 5/7 positive, with 2 Disagree.
  • Pace and switching software: “…not have it feel like rush crash course making it feel discouraging when someone gets left behind or not following as fast. stay on one gaming programs and follow a course for it for longer…”
  • Comfort asking questions: “…asking questions at times makes me dumb for asking and feeling im now slowing the class down, when we are in a introduction class.”
  • Organization and technical interruptions: “…activities for each class often feel disjointed, and lose track of time as a result of unexpected technical issues/troubleshooting with hardware/software…”
  • Structure and tutorials: “DMA 10 could benefit from a slightly more rigid structure in terms of week to week assignments.” · “…have more videos that are tied to the lesson of the week for extra help,”
  • Item 7 (more than one explanation) was 6/7 with 1 Disagree. Item 3 (exams reflect material) was 6/7. 0 of 7 respondents had visited office hours.

REFLECTION — WHY DMA 10 DIFFERS (DRAFT FOR PROFESSOR OCEAN TO REVIEW)

Drafted from the evidence on this site, in the faculty member's voice. Edit before submitting. Each statement links to its source.

My DMA 15 and DMA 40 respondents were positive on every item. DMA 10 respondents were also unanimous on preparation, engagement, responsiveness and interest in their success, but three of seven disagreed that I presented the subject matter clearly, and two disagreed that my feedback was timely. I take those results seriously. They agree with what one student described in detail, and with Carolyn Castaño's October 27 observation, which rated communicating "ideas, instructions, assignments, and other presentations effectively, clearly, and accurately" as Needs Improvement while rating the observation overall Satisfactory (observation).

Several things make DMA 10 different. It is an introductory course, and my own syllabus plans seven different tools across sixteen weeks: Blender, PICO-8, Substance Painter, Roblox Studio, Minecraft Education, Unity and Unreal Engine 5 (DMA 10 case study). Beginners met a new interface every few weeks, which matches the comment asking me to "stay on one gaming programs." The class also met in LAC-K143, where, as Ms. Castaño recorded, workstations face the wall, the instructor station is far from one side of the room, and the acoustics "are not great." I don't treat the room as the explanation for the clarity results. It is part of the context I need to plan around.

The positive comments are just as specific: patience, explaining "why we learn certain things," in-class catch-up time and case-by-case technical help. I want to keep those strengths while making the course easier to follow. With 7 of 22 students responding, I treat these results as a signal rather than a verdict on the whole class.

Sources: the three Individual Reports (pp. 3–5); Castaño E-2.A (pp. 1–3); DMA 10 syllabus weekly outline (Oct 12 file).
