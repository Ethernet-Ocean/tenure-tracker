APPENDIX E-3 — RESPONSE TO STUDENT EVALUATIONS

The three questions below are the questions on the official Appendix E-3 form (Probationary Faculty Response to Student Evaluation Form, LBCCFA-LBCCD Master Agreement 2026–2029).

Q1 · What did you learn about your professional performance from your student evaluation forms? Q2 · What adjustments do you plan to make as a result of these evaluations? Q3 · What adjustments have you made as a result of previous evaluations?
About the data. Fall 2026 Individual Reports (created Nov 16, 2026): DMA 10 had 7 of 22 students respond (31.82%), DMA 15 7 of 25 (28%), and DMA 40 3 of 13 (23.08%). Responses are anonymous and the samples are small, so they describe only the students who responded. "Positive" means Strongly Agree or Agree. Every quotation below is copied exactly, including the student's spelling.

REFLECTION — WHY DMA 10 DIFFERS (DRAFT FOR PROFESSOR OCEAN TO REVIEW)

Drafted from the evidence on this site, in the faculty member's voice. Edit before submitting. Each statement links to its source.

My DMA 15 and DMA 40 respondents were positive on every item. DMA 10 respondents were also unanimous on preparation, engagement, responsiveness and interest in their success, but three of seven disagreed that I presented the subject matter clearly, and two disagreed that my feedback was timely. I take those results seriously. They agree with what one student described in detail, and with Carolyn Castaño's October 27 observation, which rated communicating "ideas, instructions, assignments, and other presentations effectively, clearly, and accurately" as Needs Improvement while rating the observation overall Satisfactory (observation report).

Several things make DMA 10 different. It is an introductory course, and my own syllabus plans seven different tools across sixteen weeks: Blender, PICO-8, Substance Painter, Roblox Studio, Minecraft Education, Unity and Unreal Engine 5 (DMA 10 focus). Beginners met a new interface every few weeks, which matches the comment asking me to "stay on one gaming programs." The class also met in LAC-K143, where, as Ms. Castaño recorded, workstations face the wall, the instructor station is far from one side of the room, and the acoustics "are not great." I don't treat the room as the explanation for the clarity results. It is part of the context I need to plan around.

The positive comments are just as specific: patience, explaining "why we learn certain things," in-class catch-up time and case-by-case technical help. I want to keep those strengths while making the course easier to follow. With 7 of 22 students responding, I treat these results as a signal rather than a verdict on the whole class.

Sources: the three Individual Reports (pp. 3–5); Castaño E-2.A (pp. 1–3); DMA 10 syllabus weekly outline (Oct 12 file).

Q2 · WHAT ADJUSTMENTS DO I PLAN TO MAKE?

DMA 10 FOCUS — STRENGTHS, CLASSROOM CONSTRAINTS AND RESPONSIVE IMPROVEMENT

Sources: Castaño E-2.A (observed Oct 27, signed Nov 8); DMA 10 Individual Report; CRN 71864 Canvas export (Aug 25); five syllabus versions; Sep 30 playtest guide. The Oct 27 lesson-plan files have not been provided.

1 · STUDENT CONCERNS

7 of 22 students responded. Item 6 (clear presentation) was 4/7 positive, 3 Disagree, and item 9 (timely feedback) 5/7, 2 Disagree. Comments named pace, frequent software changes, "disjointed" activities, technical interruptions, instructions hard to see, discomfort asking questions, and a wish for lesson-linked videos. Details

2 · THE LESSON PLAN

The Oct 27 Unity Physics Challenge lesson-plan files have not been provided. Several versions reportedly exist, so this page doesn't describe one. What was taught is documented only by the observer (part 3). The syllabus places "Game Engine Physics" in Week 9 (Oct 26 – Nov 1).

3 · OBSERVED STRENGTHS

Castaño recorded: a Kahoot warm-up recalling earlier concepts, with missed questions reviewed and students explaining why answers were correct; student presentations of marble games "created with a creator kit", where students explained "what changes they made and how the game worked" with guided peer response; feedback-sandwich and judgment-free-zone critique norms; lab work with an embedded tutor. "Professor Oceano was well organized, engaging, and approachable."

4 · THE COMMUNICATION FINDING

Criterion "Communicates ideas, instructions, assignments, and other presentations effectively, clearly, and accurately": Needs Improvement. The narrative says "At times it was difficult to hear Professor Oceano" and that some students kept looking at their computers during the Kahoot and the demonstrations. The overall observation was Satisfactory.

5 · ROOM LAYOUT AND ACOUSTICS

Recorded by the observer: K143 "has computer stations facing the wall, while the instructor is at the front"; "the instructor's workstation is far from one side of the classroom"; the acoustics "are not great." She noted the layout "creates a situation where some students do not always pay attention to the instructor."

6 · RELEVANT UDL PRACTICES

Verified: UDL tutorial credit (syllabus p. 8); flexible submission formats (Canvas); Kahoot with re-explanation; structured peer critique; an embedded tutor. These address representation and engagement, but the communication finding shows live instruction still needs work. UDL table

7 · ACTIONS ACTUALLY TAKEN

None documented yet. The observation was discussed Nov 6 and signed Nov 8, and the evaluations arrived Nov 16. The observer states the instructor "is already scheduled to teach next semester in the library classroom". That still needs confirming in the Spring 2027 schedule.

8 · IMPROVEMENTS PLANNED

  • Project the voice or use a microphone; ask Facilities/ITS about audio
  • Walk to the far side of the room; gathered check-in before lab work
  • Agenda and written step lists for each demo
  • Fewer tool changes per unit, plus lesson-linked tutorials
  • A published grading-turnaround target

All planned. Matrix

9 · HOW IMPROVEMENT WILL BE JUDGED

  • Spring 2027 DMA 10 evaluations, items 6, 7 and 9, compared with Fall 2026's 4/7, 6/7 and 5/7 (noting small samples)
  • The criterion 2 rating in the next in-person observation

  • Canvas gradebook dates against the published turnaround target

DMA 10 COURSE DESIGN — WHAT THE DOCUMENTS SHOW

VERIFIED: 16-WEEK MODULE DESIGN

The Canvas export of CRN 71864 contains 21 modules: a Start Here module, 4 units (Foundations & Game Art · Game Art & Mechanics · Gameplay & Playtesting · Prototyping, Publishing & Portfolio) and 16 weekly modules. Each week has a Discover page, a Discuss discussion and a Demonstrate assignment. All were marked published at export.

"Deepen" appears as a module link ("Deepen – Video Tutorial Library") and in the syllabus sequence "Discover → Discuss → Demonstrate → Deepen → Publish". It is not a separate weekly item.

LIMITS OF THIS EVIDENCE

  • The export is dated Aug 25, before the term began (Aug 31 / Sep 1). It shows the planned shell, not what students saw later.
  • At export, every weekly Demonstrate assignment appeared twice (20- and 25-point versions), there were 16 untitled placeholder assignments, no due dates were set, and the only rubric was a discussion rubric. Whether this was cleaned up before students used the course is unverified.
  • Five syllabus versions differ in grade weights (35/20/15/15/15 vs 55/20/15/10), office hours, and start date (Aug 31 vs Sep 1). Two are labelled "TEST SYLLABUS". Which version was distributed is unverified.
