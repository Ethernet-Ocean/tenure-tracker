DMA 10 ROBLOX OBBY

DMA 15 Playtest Feedback & Revision Guide

Purpose: Students in DMA 15 playtested your Roblox obstacle courses from a user-experience perspective. Use this feedback as evidence for iteration—not as a grade. Focus on patterns that appeared across multiple testers, then make one or two meaningful improvements.

Classwide Playtest Patterns

  • Checkpoint placement: Several testers wanted more checkpoints so difficult sections did not require restarting the entire course.
  • Clarity & wayfinding: Players should quickly understand where to go, what counts as success, and whether a platform is a checkpoint or the finish.
  • Beginner accessibility: Some DMA 15 testers were new to Roblox. Platform spacing, jump precision, camera visibility, and clear onboarding strongly affected their experience.
  • Readable feedback: Instructions, color contrast, enemy cues, objectives, and visual indicators can make the intended action easier to understand.
  • Intentional difficulty: Challenge can be fun when it feels purposeful. Repeated random deaths, long falls, or unclear hazards can feel frustrating instead.

Project Feedback

Aaron — Game Design Obby

What players liked

  • Environment features and effects were memorable.
  • Several testers found the path/objective understandable once they were oriented.
  • The opening platform gave newer players time to learn before failing.

Best revision priorities

  • Increase instruction size/visibility and strengthen color contrast.
  • Reduce visual strain from flashing background effects.
  • Check camera visibility, jump feel, and the delayed-feeling third-arrow hitbox.
  • Strengthen the ending/Lancer interaction and make sure players spawn facing the course.

Test next: Have one first-time Roblox player attempt the course without help. Watch whether they see the instructions, understand the path, and successfully trigger the ending.

Alberto — The Greatest Obby to Ever Do It

What players liked

  • The opening challenge communicates a deliberately difficult, high-energy style.
  • Testers recognized the frustration as largely intentional challenge.
  • The course concept was memorable and playful.

Best revision priorities

  • Clearly label the large green area as a checkpoint rather than a finish.
  • Make the continuation after the checkpoint more visible.
  • Consider slightly more forgiving platform sizing/spacing for beginners.

Test next: Ask testers to identify the checkpoint and next destination without verbal guidance. If most continue immediately, the visual hierarchy is working.

Brisa — Course

What players liked

  • Visual design received strong praise; one tester described it as “so pretty.”
  • The course reads as an obstacle course to many players.
  • The challenge can be engaging for experienced platform players.

Best revision priorities

  • Revisit the first rotating platform and precise jump spacing.
  • Clarify what is damaging the player near checkpoints.
  • Reduce long fall time after failure and consider more forgiving checkpoint placement.

Test next: Test with both an experienced Roblox player and a beginner. Compare where each fails and whether either can explain why they died.

Carlos — Obstacle Course

What players liked

  • Players generally understood the objective.
  • The theme and finish line were positively received.
  • The difficult “hellscape” feeling matched the visual design, making challenge feel intentional to some testers.

Best revision priorities

  • Add checkpoints, especially before/after demanding sections.
  • Review platform spacing and jump difficulty for less experienced players.
  • Keep the difficulty, but reduce repetition after a fall.

Test next: Time how long it takes a new player to recover after a death. A checkpoint should preserve challenge while reducing unnecessary replay.

Jose — Pirate Obby! ARGHHH

What players liked

  • The pirate theme and rising difficulty gave the course personality.
  • Reachable platforms and checkpoints were generally visible.
  • The obstacle-course structure was understandable once players got started.

Best revision priorities

  • Signal earlier that pirates are enemies (dialogue, animation, warning, or visual cue).
  • Clarify the purpose of objects such as the rainbow path and boards on the octopus arms.
  • Prevent the starting pirate/flag from knocking out new players before they understand the level.
  • Consider a checkpoint after the pirate encounter.

Test next: Give a first-time player 30 seconds at the opening. Ask them what is dangerous, where they should go, and what they think each interactive object does.

Mason — TEST

What players liked

  • The character structures/models were visually interesting and a creative idea for an environment.
  • Some players enjoyed having to explore and figure out the course.
  • The open space gave the project a distinctive feel.

Best revision priorities

  • Make it immediately clear that the character models form the obstacle course.
  • Add a visible start, route, and finish/objective.
  • Use instructions or environmental cues so players know where to jump next.
  • Check whether different avatar sizes can reach required platforms.

Test next: Show the game to someone without explaining it. If they can identify the start, route, and goal within 10–15 seconds, the objective is clear enough.

Sam — The One Who Jumps

What players liked

  • Testers liked the humor, moving obstacles, and breaks between platforming sections.
  • The overall course direction was easy to understand.
  • The chase sequence and unusual obstacles made the experience memorable.

Best revision priorities

  • Add a checkpoint before/around the spinning-wheel or harder sequence.
  • Limit repeated random deaths in the three-door section so players do not get stuck indefinitely.
  • Make chase images readable from more camera angles.
  • Consider a little more support for players with limited platforming experience.

Test next: Count how many consecutive deaths a tester experiences in the three-door sequence and whether they stay engaged. Adjust randomness/checkpoints if repetition becomes the main challenge.
