Environment Design, Part 2

Landscape • Post Process Volume • Niagara VFX

DMA 40 | Advanced Multimedia • Professor Ocean • UE5 • 200-minute class

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE Transform the Part 1 terrain concept into an atmospheric, readable, story-driven Unreal Engine 5 environment. Every design change should serve a player experience.

Success criteria students can see

  • LANDSCAPE — Refine at least one purposeful terrain feature (path, elevation, slope, ridge, or focal point) using UE5 Landscape tools.
  • POST PROCESS — Add a Post Process Volume, enable it for the level, and adjust at least two visible settings (for example, color grading and bloom).
  • NIAGARA — Create or adapt one Niagara system, place it in the environment, and change at least one effect parameter.
  • DESIGN RATIONALE — Explain how terrain, atmosphere, and VFX guide attention, establish mood, or communicate story.

Live teaching guide | terrain + atmosphere

OPENING SCRIPT “Last class we built the space. Today we design how it feels. Good environment design uses shape, light, color, and motion to communicate something before a player reads a single word.”

1 | UE5 Landscape tools

Demonstrate on a prepared UE5 level, then let students immediately apply the tool to their own scene.

1. Open the existing UE5 level (or a prebuilt UE5 starter landscape). Save As a Part 2 working copy before editing.

2. Open Select Mode → Landscape (shortcut Shift + 2). Choose the existing Landscape actor, or create a simple landscape if needed.

3. Select Sculpt. Raise/lower terrain to support a design idea: ridge, valley, elevated lookout, or gently guided route.

4. Use Smooth to reduce harsh edges; use Flatten for a playable landing, stage, or platform. Keep traversal readable.

5. Optional only: Paint an additional surface material IF the landscape material has configured paint layers/layer info. Do not make material authoring today’s bottleneck.

6. Return to Select mode and inspect the landscape from a player-height viewpoint. Save.

Ask aloud: “Where should the player go first? What terrain shape makes that clear?”

CHECKPOINT 1 — ABOUT 10:50 Each student points to one deliberate terrain change and states its purpose in a sentence. Listen for “guides movement,” “creates scale,” or “reveals a landmark.”

2 | Post Process Volume

1. Open the Place Actors panel (or Add menu), search “Post Process Volume,” and place one into the level.

2. Select the volume. Under Post Process Volume Settings, check Infinite Extent (Unbound) so it affects the full level. (A bounded volume is an advanced alternative.)

3. In Details, find Color Grading. Enable an override checkbox for Saturation or Contrast, then make a modest visible change that supports the intended mood.

4. Find Bloom (under Lens settings, depending on UE5 version). Enable its override and adjust Intensity gently. If exposure is changing unexpectedly, review Exposure settings rather than cranking bloom.

5. Toggle the volume’s Enabled control or its specific override checkbox to compare before/after. Explain why the chosen look makes the player experience clearer.

6. Rename the actor PPV_GlobalMood and save the level.

Ask aloud: “Is a cold, muted color grade helping us tell a different story than a warm, saturated one?”

CHECKPOINT 2 — ABOUT 11:35 Students show the volume in the Outliner and identify TWO modified post-process settings. Ask partners to describe what changed, not simply say “looks cool.”

Instructor watch-outs

  • A Post Process Volume has no visible effect if relevant setting overrides are not enabled; use the Details search bar to locate settings quickly.
  • An unbound volume affects the entire world, independent of physical size. More volumes can override or blend with one another.
  • Do not assume a UEFN project opens as a standard UE5 project. Carry forward the environment DESIGN from Part 1; use a prepared UE5 level or recreate the terrain in UE5.

Live teaching guide | Niagara + guided studio

3 | Niagara VFX system

Focus on one ambient effect that makes the environment feel alive: dust, mist-like particles, embers, floating motes, or magical energy. Avoid complex fluid simulations for this session.

1. Open Content Drawer / Content Browser; right-click in the project Content folder → FX → Niagara System (menu placement may vary).

2. Create a system from an available template or emitter, such as Fountain (continuous motion) or Simple Sprite Burst (one-shot effect). If the wizard shows “New system from selected emitters,” choose the template and add it to the system.

3. Name it NS_EnvironmentMood, open it, and locate the emitter stack. Briefly distinguish System → Emitter → Modules.

4. Change at least ONE visible setting, choosing what the template exposes: Spawn Rate/count, particle size, lifetime, color, or velocity. Preview the difference.

5. Drag the Niagara System from Content Browser into the level. Position and scale/adjust effect parameters so it enhances, rather than hides, the focal point.

6. Play or Simulate the scene to test visibility, motion and readability. Save the Niagara asset and the level.

Ask aloud: “Would this effect help a player notice danger, weather, magic, or movement? Where should it be placed?”

CHECKPOINT 3 — BY 12:45 Student shows the Niagara actor in the level and points to one edited emitter/system setting. Ask them to explain the visual purpose of the effect.

During independent studio

  • Circulate in three quick rounds: first students needing a starter, then workflow questions, then design critique.
  • Use “show me / tell me / next step” feedback: “Show me your volume. Tell me the mood. What one tweak would improve readability?”
  • Offer visible written steps and a live demonstration; students can work solo or compare ideas with a partner.
  • If a system crashes or a template is missing, students can demonstrate the next step on a teacher-provided sample level and submit a progress screenshot with a brief explanation; follow up on completing the technical requirement.

QUICK FIXES If sculpting fails, check the Landscape mode and selected actor. If the grade is unchanged, enable the PPV and setting overrides. If VFX is invisible, check Niagara playback, spawn behavior and placement.

Student-facing assignment | copy into Canvas

Demonstrate: Environment Design, Part 2 — Atmosphere & VFX

In Part 1, you established the foundation of your virtual environment. In Part 2, use Unreal Engine 5 to make the space more intentional and immersive. Your goal is to communicate a clear mood, story, or player experience through terrain, post processing and motion.

What you will make

1. Landscape: change one terrain feature with UE5 Landscape tools (examples: sculpt a ridge, smooth a path, flatten a platform).

2. Post Process Volume: add one to the level and adjust at least TWO visible properties, such as saturation/contrast and bloom.

3. Niagara VFX: add one Niagara System to the level and edit at least ONE parameter.

4. Creative explanation: write 2–3 sentences explaining how your terrain, color and effect support the same design goal.

Choose a theme (or invent your own)

Mysterious forest • Desert ruins • Alien landscape • Volcanic valley • Frozen overlook • Your own concept

Exactly what to submit

CHOOSE ONE SUBMISSION FORMAT OPTION A: Three labeled screenshots — (1) terrain edit, (2) your Post Process Volume/settings or clear before-and-after comparison, and (3) Niagara effect in the scene plus the parameter you changed. Add a 2–3 sentence design rationale. OPTION B: A 20–45 second screen recording showing those SAME three requirements, plus the 2–3 sentence written rationale.

Submit to this Canvas assignment as file attachments or a working viewable link. If you use a link, check that the instructor can open it. You may also attach your UE5 project folder as a ZIP if feasible; this is optional and does not replace the required visual evidence.

Your work does not need to be photorealistic. It does need to show intentional choices and the required tool use.

Assessment rubric | 20 points

CRITERION

4 POINTS: COMPLETE EVIDENCE

PARTIAL EVIDENCE

Terrain / 4

Purposeful UE5 Landscape edit is visible and explained.

Tool used, but change or purpose is unclear.

Niagara / 4

Submission / 4

Chosen format shows all three elements and 2–3 sentence rationale.

Evidence or rationale is incomplete.

Instructor note: Use the rubric consistently for both formats; a stronger visual style or optional extension should not substitute for meeting the core learning outcomes.

Teacher’s desk checklist

  • Before class: open/test the UE5 project on a LAB computer; verify Niagara templates load; prepare a small landscape, PPV demo and backup files.
  • Display today’s objectives and submission instructions on Canvas BEFORE students begin.
  • Save BEFORE/AFTER screenshots for the opening comparison; keep a sample completed scene ready.
  • At each checkpoint, ask for visible proof and a brief explanation—not just “Is everybody okay?”
  • Keep one example from each pathway for your own teaching reflection (consistent with campus/privacy rules).
  • Close with the exit ticket and tell students when/how to finish or resubmit unfinished work.

Exit ticket | last 5 minutes

  • Which UE5 tool changed the PLAYER EXPERIENCE most today? Why?
  • What did you change in your Post Process Volume?
  • What one Niagara property did you edit, and what is your next step?

Post-observation reflection prompts

What proportion of students demonstrated each success criterion? Where did students need support? What specific instruction, example or accessibility improvement will I adjust next class? Document one student design choice that shows transfer from UEFN terrain planning to UE5 environmental storytelling.
