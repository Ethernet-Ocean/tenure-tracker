DMA 10 | Introduction to Game Design

Unity Physics Challenge: Roll to the Target

INSTRUCTOR Professor Oceano Marshall • OBSERVER Carolyn Castaño

Tuesday, October 27, 2026 • 2:00–5:10 PM • Room K143 • Unity 3D (no programming required)

Lesson overview

Students build a compact physics challenge using Unity primitives. They construct a ramp, release a ball using Rigidbody physics, and iterate until the ball reaches a marked target. This emphasizes cause and effect, collision behavior, playtesting, and intentional design decisions.

Learning outcomes

  • Build a small 3D scene with colliders and a Rigidbody component.
  • Explain how gravity, ramp angle, and object placement influence an in-engine outcome.
  • Run, observe, revise, and communicate one improvement based on playtesting.

Simple challenge parameters (student-facing)

REQUIRED ELEMENT

WHAT STUDENTS MUST SHOW

1 Play area

A visible ground/platform and at least one tilted ramp made with 3D objects.

2 Physics object

One Sphere with Rigidbody (Use Gravity on) and a collider.

3 Target

A clearly marked landing zone or goal (color, shape, or label).

4 Test and revise

Press Play, observe the ball, change ONE factor (e.g., ramp angle or ball placement), and test again.

5 Evidence

Show the working scene and describe what changed and why (spoken, 2 sentences, or screenshots).

Success criteria: The ball moves through physics, collides with the scene, and students can explain a tested design change. Reaching the target is the goal, not a prerequisite for full participation.

STUDENT HANDOUT | UNITY PHYSICS CHALLENGE

Can You Roll to the Target?

Mission: Make a ball travel down a ramp toward a marked goal using Unity’s built-in physics. Keep it simple, test your idea, and make one smart adjustment.

Build checklist

  • Create a new 3D Unity scene with a ground Plane (or scaled Cube).
  • Add a Cube; rotate it to create a visible ramp. Keep the ramp over or near the ground.
  • Add a Sphere above the ramp. In Inspector, select Add Component → Rigidbody and leave Use Gravity on.
  • Check the objects have colliders (Unity 3D primitives usually include these by default).
  • Make a clearly visible goal/landing zone with a differently colored object or material.
  • Press Play. Observe what happens, then stop Play mode before making permanent changes.
  • Change ONE thing (ramp slope, starting point, target location, or obstacle placement) and test again.

Choose one bonus (optional)

  • Add a simple wall or obstacle that makes the ball redirect or stop.
  • Use materials or color to communicate start, ramp, and finish clearly.
  • Try a second ramp or bouncing surface; explain the different response.

Show your learning

Demonstrate your scene in Play mode, explain your one change, and upload 1–2 screenshots (or a brief screen recording) to the Canvas Demonstrate activity. A short spoken explanation or written response is equally acceptable.

Instructor observation & support

Formative checks: “Which object needs Rigidbody?” “What is the collider doing?” “What one change produced a different result?” Monitor testing, design reasoning, and peer feedback—not just a perfect final landing.

Access and UDL: live modeling; visible checklist; peer collaboration; self-paced replay of the linked video; optional written or verbal explanation; adjustable challenge difficulty. No coding required. If learners finish early, offer a creative extension.
